Volunteer Program — Outreach & Recruitment Plan

The board runs every program itself with no dedicated staff. This is the plan to build a real volunteer pipeline — starting with high school and university outreach this week — so the load stops sitting on a handful of people.

01 · Who we're recruiting, and why they'd say yes

On-program

Coaching & event-day help

Assisting Learn-to-Skate, hockey, basketball, and ad-hoc events. Good fit for high schoolers needing volunteer hours and university kinesiology/sports management students wanting coaching experience.

Skills-based

Finance & reporting help

A business/commerce student could help build and maintain the program P&L tracking from the roadmap's Phase 4 — real resume-worthy experience, and it takes a build task off the board's plate.

Skills-based

Social & comms help

A marketing/communications student could run the social accounts described in Phase 6 of the roadmap, using the brand guide as their reference.

Why this matters beyond extra hands: two of these three roles map directly onto workstreams the board doesn't have time for (finance, social). Recruiting for skill-matched roles, not just on-ice help, is what actually reduces the 2–3 hr/week bottleneck described in the roadmap.

03 · Outreach templates

To: High school guidance counselor / volunteer-hours coordinatorSubject: Volunteer opportunities for your students
Hi [Name], I'm reaching out from the Korean Canadian Athletic Association (KCAA), a community non-profit running youth sports programs including learn-to-skate, hockey, and basketball. We're looking for high school volunteers to help with on-ice assistance, event-day support, and program admin — work that can count toward [your school's / the province's community-hours requirement]. Roles are flexible, a few hours on select weekends, and a good fit for students interested in coaching, sports management, or just giving back. Would you be open to sharing this with interested students, or pointing me to the right contact for volunteer postings at [School Name]? I've attached our intro material with more detail. Thanks, [Your name], KCAA info@kcaa.ca · kcaa.ca
To: University co-op office / Korean Students' AssociationSubject: Volunteer & experience opportunities with KCAA
Hi [Name], I'm with the Korean Canadian Athletic Association (KCAA), a non-profit running youth and adult sports programs (hockey, learn-to-skate, basketball, and more) in [city]. We're an all-volunteer board building out a student volunteer program this year. Beyond on-program help, we're especially interested in students who'd want hands-on experience alongside volunteering — for example: · Business/commerce students helping with basic financial tracking and reporting · Marketing/communications students helping run our social media · Kinesiology/sports management students assisting with coaching and program delivery Would it be possible to share this with your members, or point me to your volunteer/co-op coordinator? I've attached our intro material with more detail. Thanks, [Your name], KCAA info@kcaa.ca · kcaa.ca

Both templates lean on the voice rules in the brand guide, section 08 — plain-spoken, community-first, no jargon. Attach or link the recruitment intro material with both — it covers who we are, what we run, and the same four role categories in more depth than an email can.

04 · Intake

Intro material

The recruitment intro material is what actually gets attached or linked in outreach — who we are, what we run, why volunteer, and the four role categories, ending in a direct email CTA.

Google Form

Name, contact info, school/program, availability, role interest (on-program / finance / social / other), and any relevant experience. Not built yet — the intro material's CTA defaults to email until this exists.

Response routing

Once the form exists, responses feed straight into the tracker below — no separate inbox to check, which matters until Phase 3's inbox automation exists.

05 · Outreach tracker

Live tracker, not a static sheet

Summary by milestone at the top, every school's stage and contact details in the middle, and a form at the bottom to log new outreach — shared live with the whole board.

Open the tracker →

Stages run Not Contacted → Contacted → In Conversation → Agreed to Partner → Active Partner, plus a separate Declined state. A school counts as an Active Partner once the poster is up and/or volunteers have been sent — whichever comes first.
